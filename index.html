BandWatch HF

A space-weather dashboard that tells a ham operator which HF bands are open right now, on a live world map.

  • Propagation model: solar flux to sunspot number to foF2, blended by sun elevation into MUF and LUF per band

02Case study · 2025–2026

Zummo Commerce System

An audit-logged operations system that tracked every donated bike from intake to sale, and every skill a young mechanic showed along the way. It ran in a real shop on real inventory.

Bike lifecycle
Intake → Repair → Senior sign-off → Pricing → Label → Ready to sell → Sold → Archived

What I built

  • Next.js 16 App Router with Server Actions, Drizzle ORM over SQLite on a Railway volume, migrated off a broken Postgres setup
  • Gated, audit-logged bike lifecycle with senior sign-off before anything can be priced or sold
  • Drag-and-drop label editor for Brother DK-1201 tags with Code 128 barcodes
  • Square OAuth: printing a tag creates the Square item; a bridge polls Square every 15 minutes and marks bikes sold
  • Square reconciliation that pre-fills a match but never auto-submits
  • Read-only bike feed and sold list so the shop's AI listing assistant never re-types details
  • Historical import of 40 real bikes, CSV export and 18 automated tests

How I led it

  • Wrote the ZCS Plan of Attack leadership approved: voice entry, AI tools, milestones and a build crew of younger shop staff
  • Moved the codebase into the shop's own GitHub organization so the work belongs to Zummo, not to me
  • After a Nov 7 ops meeting, turned the team's asks into a master spec: data-loss fix, richer intake, sold tracking, slow-seller and wheel-size reports, mechanic skill pages, a help guide and QR reference sheet
  • Caught and fixed a privacy bug that let students see other students' records

Skills as data

ZCS was designed to double as a training record. AI captures the work and asks questions, a senior mechanic verifies it, and a deterministic engine awards levels, so every credential is defensible to a school district or a parent.

Bike → Work performed → Mechanic → Skill shown → Senior verifies → Credential → Customer record

What happened, and what I learned

In November 2026 the shop consolidated onto Muse, its AI assistant. My bike workflow, tag printing and Square bridge were moved over in two days, and leadership said everything I built integrated well. The shop now runs on dictation and those labels.

The lesson: fit the workflow before adding rules, and build clean data with an AI layer you can swap, never the other way around.

06How I work

Principles I learned by shipping

Fit the workflow first
A very structured system on a very unstructured shop won't stick. ZCS taught me that the hard way.
Clean data, swappable AI
Build the data model to last and keep the AI as an interface layer you can change when a better model shows up.
Humans confirm, software records
Reconciliation pre-fills but never auto-submits. AI can capture work; a senior mechanic verifies it.
Report the real number
If a model loses to its baseline, the write-up says so. If a tool has limits, the report lists them.
